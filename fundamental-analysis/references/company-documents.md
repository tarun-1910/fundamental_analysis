# Company-Published Documents: What to Read and What to Extract

Company filings are primary sources. Screeners and news summaries are secondary and can be wrong or lag behind, so use them to find leads and then verify against the documents below.

## 1. Annual report (read the latest 3 if possible)

| Section | What to extract |
|---|---|
| Chairman / MD letter | Strategy, tone, whether last year's promises were met |
| Management Discussion & Analysis (MD&A) | Segment performance, outlook, risks as management sees them, capex plans |
| Directors' report | Dividend policy, related-party summary, changes in capital, board changes |
| Corporate governance report | Board independence, committee composition, promoter holding, remuneration |
| **Auditor's report** | Opinion type (qualified/adverse is a serious flag), **Key Audit Matters**, going-concern remarks, emphasis-of-matter paragraphs |
| Financial statements | Multi-year income statement, balance sheet, cash flow |
| **Notes to accounts** | Revenue recognition policy and changes, contingent liabilities, **related-party transactions**, debt maturity and security, pledged assets, capital commitments, segment data, exceptional items, deferred tax, impairment, employee stock options |
| BRSR / sustainability report | Environmental or compliance risks, litigation, workforce data |

Notes to accounts are where most problems hide. Do not skip them.

## 2. Quarterly results and filings

- Revenue, EBITDA, net profit versus the same quarter last year (YoY) and the previous quarter (QoQ)
- Segment-wise results, exceptional items and other income
- Auditor's limited review remarks
- Shareholding pattern each quarter: promoter holding, **pledged shares**, FII/DII/public changes
- Look for trend breaks: margin compression, receivables jumps, rising finance costs

## 3. Investor presentations

- Management's own KPIs (volumes, order book, same-store growth, ARPU, etc.)
- Guidance and targets, which can be tracked against later results
- Capex and capacity plans, market share claims
- Treat as marketing: verify claims against the audited numbers

## 4. Earnings call transcripts

- Analyst questions that management dodges or answers vaguely
- Changes in tone or guidance versus previous calls
- Demand commentary, pricing, input cost pressures, competitive moves
- Details missing from the formal documents (order inflow, client losses, regulatory conversations)

## 5. Exchange disclosures and regulatory filings

- Announcements: acquisitions, fundraising, credit rating actions, litigation, regulatory orders, auditor or key-management resignations, delayed results
- Credit rating reports (CRISIL, ICRA, CARE, India Ratings): independent view of leverage and liquidity, with stated rating rationale and sensitivities
- For US-listed companies: 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, proxy statement (DEF 14A) on SEC EDGAR

## 6. Prospectus / DRHP (for recent listings or limited history)

- Objects of the issue (how the money will be used), promoter background, litigation section, risk factors, related-party dealings, historical financials

## 7. How to find them

1. Company website, "Investors" section: annual reports, results, presentations, call transcripts and audio.
2. BSE (bseindia.com) and NSE (nseindia.com): corporate announcements, results, shareholding patterns, annual reports.
3. SEC EDGAR (sec.gov) for US companies; the relevant regulator or exchange elsewhere.
4. Rating agency websites for credit rating rationales.
5. If a document cannot be fetched or is behind a login, say so in the output and name what was used instead.

## 8. Reading approach and cross-checks

- Start with the auditor's report and notes to accounts, then MD&A, then the statements. This order exposes problems before the narrative colors your view.
- Cross-check: MD&A claims vs reported numbers; presentation KPIs vs audited figures; cash flow statement vs profit; related-party notes vs governance report.
- Compare the latest report to the one three years back: has the business, the debt, the auditor, or the shareholding pattern changed?
- Quote figures with the source and period (for example "FY25 annual report, note 32"). Do not reproduce long passages from documents; summarize in your own words and point to the section.
- Record which documents you actually read, and list any you could not access, in the "Sources and data gaps" section of the output.
